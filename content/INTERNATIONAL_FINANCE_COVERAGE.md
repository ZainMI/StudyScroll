## Source inconsistencies and conventions

- **Chapter 4 PDF p.21:** Figure 4.5 plots a rise in interest following an income increase at fixed real money supply, but its caption says “reduces.” Cards use the graph and M/P=L(R,Y): interest **rises**.
- **Chapter 3 PDF pp.13–14:** One prose sentence reverses the sweater/jeans relative-price interpretation. Cards use the explicit currency conversion and the table: E×P£/P$ rises with dollars per pound.
- **Chapter 2:** Early slides use simplified CA≈EX−IM; later slides include income and transfers. Cards distinguish the full current account from goods trade and distinguish transfers from newly produced output/disposable resources.
- **Financial-account signs:** The slides' identity uses net inflows, CA+KA+FA=0. The detailed U.S. table also displays net acquisition-minus-liabilities measures. Do not insert an outflow-positive number into an inflow-positive identity without reversing its sign.
- **Wealth changes:** CA is not generally the entire measured NIIP change; price, currency, capital-transfer, and other adjustments matter. The homework's stronger equality is a simplified accounting exercise.
- **Exact versus approximate returns:** Additive UIP/CIP and inflation-difference formulas omit cross-products. Numerical cards explicitly label both when relevant. The Chapter 3 p.58 covered-return example gives exactly 10.24% from the displayed inputs, rather than the slide's roughly 10.3%; approximate CIP alone hides this gap.
- **Chapter 2 homework Q10 row 8:** CA and G are both missing. The supplied values imply CA=220−G, not unique numbers. No missing value was invented.
- **Chapter 3 homework Q2:** Inferring an exchange rate from jeans prices requires a one-price assumption; it does not reveal an observed market quote by itself.
- **Chapter 3 homework Q12:** Several “always/never” claims are overly strong. Cards distinguish trade date, delivery date, and maturity rather than reproduce a dubious categorical answer.
- **Chapter 4 homework Q3:** The offered “EXCEPT” choices mix physical loss, inflation, and confiscation. No official key is supplied. Cards teach the slide's principal purchasing-power risk rather than asserting that confiscation can never happen.
- **Chapter 4 homework Q19:** The claimed Canadian historical episode has no supporting date or data here. The card teaches the conditional mechanism—higher expected policy interest after inflation news—without asserting an unverified event.
- **Chapter 5 convention:** Use q=EP*/P (foreign basket relative to domestic), so q rising means domestic real depreciation. Some summary prose reverses the verbal description; equations and consistent direction checks determine the cards.
- Rates, fees, margins, tax examples, and market observations in these PDFs are classroom/historical examples, not current prices or trading recommendations. No official homework solutions were supplied; explanations are authored and checked from the model.
